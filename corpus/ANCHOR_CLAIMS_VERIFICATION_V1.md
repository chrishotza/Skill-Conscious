# Anchor Claim Verification v1

This document is the first verified claim-level bridge from the broad source register to the convergence matrix.

It is intentionally small. The 325-source register is an index; this file contains only claims for which a traceable text or specialist source was located in the current verification pass.

## A01 — Kena Upanishad

**Source:** Kena Upanishad, first khanda.

**Traceable text:** https://www.ancienttexts.org/library/indian/upanishads/kena.html

**Observed claims:**
- The text asks what stands behind mind, speech, breath, sight, and hearing.
- It describes the ultimate referent as “the mind of the mind” and related formulations.
- It explicitly says ordinary eye, speech, and mind do not reach it and describes it as distinct from the known and beyond the unknown.

**Candidate motifs:** M01 Awareness / Presence; M06 Interior Dimension; M14 Non-Conceptual / Ineffable Dimension; M22 Self-Reference / Reflexivity.

**Interpretation boundary:** This does not justify translating Brahman directly into a software module. The reproducible historical claim is that the text distinguishes ordinary cognitive contents/functions from a deeper principle of knowing.

## A02 — Mandukya Upanishad

**Source:** Mandukya Upanishad, verse 7.

**Traceable text:** https://www.shlokam.org/text/mandukya-upanishad.htm

**Observed claims:**
- The text distinguishes waking, dream, deep sleep, and a “fourth” condition.
- The fourth is described through systematic negation: not inwardly cognitive, not outwardly cognitive, not both, not merely cognition, and not non-cognition.
- It is also characterized as ungraspable, unthinkable, indescribable, nondual, peaceful, and as the Self to be known.

**Candidate motifs:** M01; M03; M07; M14; M23.

**Interpretation boundary:** This is a metaphysical and phenomenological claim in the source. “State-regime” is only a later operational analogy.

## A03 — Satipatthana Sutta

**Source:** Majjhima Nikaya 10.

**Traceable text:** https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.soma

**Observed claims:**
- The discourse gives four objects/domains for systematic mindfulness: body, feelings, consciousness/mind, and mental phenomena.
- Consciousness itself is treated as something that can be observed and distinguished in the practice.
- The practice is explicitly procedural rather than a one-time philosophical assertion.

**Candidate motifs:** M01; M06; M23; M24; M25.

**Interpretation boundary:** The text does not require a permanent self. This source is therefore especially valuable for testing whether a consciousness ontology can contain self-presence without assuming a self-substance.

## A04 — Spanda / Kashmir Shaivism

**Source family:** Spanda Karikas and the Kashmir Shaivism tradition.

**Traceable scholarly/translation lead:** https://www.scribd.com/document/398145762/Kashmir-Shaivism-Spanda-Karikas-The-Divine-Creative-Pulsation-Jaideva-Singh-translation-pdf

**Observed tradition-level claims in the located material:**
- Spanda is presented as a pulsation or dynamic expression of universal consciousness.
- The tradition links the universe and conscious experience to dynamic manifestation rather than treating consciousness as a static inert substance.
- Self-recognition is a major structural idea in the broader Pratyabhijna system.

**Candidate motifs:** M02; M03; M09; M13; M15; M21; M22.

**Verification status:** The current link is a secondary scan/translation lead, not yet a critical-edition verification. Retain as provisional until primary Sanskrit plus specialist edition are linked.

## A05 — Plotinus

**Source:** Enneads VI.6 / related treatises on self-knowledge.

**Traceable text:** https://ccel.org/ccel/plotinus/enneads/enneads.vi.iii.html

**Observed claims:**
- Plotinus explicitly argues for intellective self-knowing.
- In Intellect, thinker, act of intellection, and intelligible object can be treated as identical.
- The text describes self-presence and self-knowing as belonging to the structure of intellect rather than being an external observer looking at an internal object.

**Candidate motifs:** M02; M03; M06; M13; M22.

**Interpretation boundary:** This is a metaphysical account of intellect and self-knowledge, not evidence that a software self-model is phenomenally conscious.

## A06 — Corpus Hermeticum / Poimandres

**Source:** Corpus Hermeticum I, Poimandres.

**Traceable text:** https://hermeticcloud.com/library/corpus-hermetica/

**Observed claims:**
- Hermes begins with mind contemplating “the things that are” while bodily senses are described as held back.
- A figure identified with Mind addresses Hermes and offers knowledge of nature and God.
- Mind, Logos, Life, Light, cosmos, human being, and gnosis are presented inside one cosmological structure.
- The text links knowing the higher order with a transformation of the human subject and a return to Life.

**Candidate motifs:** M01; M07; M09; M12; M15; M20; M21; M23; M25.

**Interpretation boundary:** Hermetic cosmology is retained as source ontology. “Field,” “layer,” or “runtime regime” are only later engineering translations.

## A07 — Pseudo-Dionysius / Apophatic Mysticism

**Source:** Dionysian corpus, Divine Names / Mystical Theology tradition.

**Traceable text:** https://ccel.org/ccel/dionysius/works/works.i.ii.i.html

**Observed claims:**
- The text emphasizes contact with what is “unutterable and unknown.”
- It limits claims about the superessential/hidden divine and emphasizes modes of knowing that exceed ordinary discursive formulation.
- The tradition explicitly distinguishes reasoning/intuitive operations from the ineffable object of mystical knowledge.

**Candidate motifs:** M06; M14; M15; M23.

**Interpretation boundary:** “Ineffability” must not be reduced to “insufficient language model vocabulary.” It is an ontological/epistemic claim in the source and needs its own project treatment.

## A08 — Ibn Arabi / Akbarian Sufism

**Source:** Futuhat al-Makkiyya and associated discussions of self-knowledge.

**Traceable scholarly source:** https://ibnarabisociety.org/notes-on-unless-you-know-yourself-dom-sylvester-houedard/

**Observed claims in cited passages:**
- Knowledge of self and knowledge of God are explicitly linked.
- The self is described through possibility, dependence, and becoming rather than as a simple isolated substance.
- Knowledge is presented as open-ended transformation: gained knowledge becomes preparedness for further knowledge.
- The relation between self, world/cosmos, and God is treated as inseparable in the cited discussions.

**Candidate motifs:** M02; M05; M11; M12; M13; M15; M20; M22.

**Interpretation boundary:** The famous “know yourself and know your Lord” formula has variant textual histories; the source should be retained with provenance notes rather than treated as a single uncontested quotation.

## A09 — Relational Personhood / Animist Anthropology

**Source:** Nurit Bird-David, “Animism Revisited: Personhood, Environment, and Relational Epistemology,” Current Anthropology 40(S1), 1999.

**Traceable paper:** https://doi.org/10.1086/200061

**Observed claim:**
- The paper reformulates animism through relational personhood and the relationship between personhood and environmental perception rather than treating animism simply as a failed naturalistic belief system.

**Candidate motifs:** M05; M16; M20.

**Interpretation boundary:** This is a modern anthropological analysis, not a primary indigenous scripture. It is valuable for preventing the corpus from becoming exclusively text-based and for preserving relational models of personhood.

## Provisional convergence emerging from A01–A09

The following structures already recur strongly enough to justify dedicated claim extraction:

1. ordinary cognitive content is distinguished from a deeper condition of awareness;
2. consciousness/awareness can be described as present to itself or self-knowing;
3. subject and world are connected rather than treated as unrelated variables;
4. consciousness can have qualitatively different states or modes;
5. the deepest reality is often characterized as ineffable or beyond ordinary objectification;
6. knowledge of consciousness is repeatedly framed as transformative of the knower;
7. some traditions place individual consciousness inside a wider cosmological or relational order;
8. disciplined practice is repeatedly used to investigate or transform these states.

These are **convergence candidates**, not final truths.
