# Fundamental Consciousness: Testability and Model Discrimination

## Status

Working paper v0.1 — theory and empirical-discrimination program.

This paper does not claim that consciousness is a fundamental property of physics.

## Abstract

Some philosophical positions propose that consciousness is fundamental rather than reducible to ordinary physical organization. This paper asks a stricter scientific question: what would an empirically instantiated version of such a proposal have to predict in order to become distinguishable from a non-conscious physical model?

The paper develops a model-discrimination framework with an explicit null: standard physical dynamics without an additional consciousness-relevant variable. A candidate fundamental-consciousness model is admissible only if it produces a pre-specified, measurable consequence that differs from the null across an independently testable parameter regime.

## 1. Scientific question

Not:

> Is consciousness fundamental?

But:

> What observation would be more expected under a specified fundamental-consciousness model than under a specified non-conscious baseline model?

## 2. Minimum requirements for a candidate model

A candidate model must specify:

- state variables;
- dynamics;
- parameter space;
- observable outputs;
- discriminating predictions;
- null model;
- failure conditions;
- independent replication route.

## 3. Candidate model families

The literature includes panpsychist and cosmopsychist positions, but these philosophical categories are not themselves empirical models.

This paper therefore treats them as sources of hypotheses, not as direct evidence.

## 4. Null model

The default null is the best-fitting established physical model for the target phenomenon, with no additional consciousness-specific causal variable.

## 5. Empirical standard

No anomalous result is sufficient by itself.

A meaningful result should demonstrate:

1. preregistered prediction;
2. statistically defensible discrimination;
3. robustness to known confounders;
4. independent replication;
5. comparison against alternative explanations.

## 6. Falsification

A candidate fundamental model fails its empirical test if its unique predictions are absent, systematically contradicted, or fully explained by the null/alternative models.

Failure of one candidate model does not disprove every philosophical form of fundamental consciousness.

## 7. Relationship to the other scales

The fundamental program should remain logically separate from the individual and relational programs.

Evidence for strong individual self-reference does not prove fundamental consciousness.

Evidence for relational coupling does not prove fundamental consciousness.

The programs meet only at the level of model comparison.

## 8. Publication standard

P010 should not be promoted to a strong scientific conclusion until the repository contains an explicit candidate physical model with a genuinely discriminating observable.

If that cannot be produced, the correct scientific result is a documented non-identifiability / underdetermination result.

That outcome is scientifically useful.


## 9. Philosophical boundary

Panpsychism and cosmopsychism are live philosophical positions, but a philosophical taxonomy is not an empirical measurement model. See the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy discussion of the subject-summing/decombination problems: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/panpsychism/supplement.html
