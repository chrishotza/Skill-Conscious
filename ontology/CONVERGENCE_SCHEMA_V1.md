# Convergence Schema v1

## Principle

The repository does not rank traditions against one another.

It performs a convergence analysis.

Each source is first read in its own vocabulary. Only then are recurring structures normalized.

## Normalized motif vocabulary

M01 — Awareness / Presence: distinction between ordinary contents and awareness itself.

M02 — Self-Presence / Self-Knowing: consciousness is in some sense present to itself, knows itself, recognizes itself, or contains reflexive awareness.

M03 — Unity / Wholeness: experience or reality can appear as a unified whole rather than only a collection of independent parts.

M04 — Differentiation: distinctions such as self/other, subject/object, inner/outer, manifest/unmanifest, or knower/known.

M05 — Subject–World Relation: self and world are not unrelated datasets; their relation is ontologically or phenomenologically significant.

M06 — Interior Dimension: decisive structure is not exhausted by public behavior or externally observable contents.

M07 — Layering / Multiple Modes: consciousness or personhood can have multiple levels, states, vehicles, bodies, worlds, or modes.

M08 — Embodiment / Vehicle: consciousness is associated with a body, vital process, vehicle, breath, subtle body, organism, or other sustaining medium.

M09 — Vital / Dynamic Principle: consciousness is linked to life, movement, breath, qi, prana, spanda, nous, spirit, or another animating process.

M10 — Valence / Significance: states are not merely represented; they matter, attract, repel, bind, liberate, threaten, delight, or transform.

M11 — Temporality / Continuity: consciousness has continuity through time, changing states, cycles, memory, rebirth, awakening, death, or transformation.

M12 — Self-Transformation: realization, initiation, practice, insight, or encounter changes the subject rather than merely adding information.

M13 — Self-Recognition: a distinctive realization occurs when the subject recognizes its deeper nature or structure.

M14 — Non-Conceptual / Ineffable Dimension: ultimate or deepest conscious reality is described as beyond ordinary concepts, language, representation, or objectification.

M15 — Transpersonal / Universal Dimension: individual consciousness may participate in, arise within, express, or become identical with a larger field, mind, reality, being, or universal consciousness.

M16 — Relational / Distributed Personhood: personhood or agency may be constituted by relations to ancestors, community, land, nonhuman persons, spirits, cosmos, or other beings.

M17 — Hidden / Esoteric Structure: ordinary perception is said to conceal deeper causal or ontological structures accessible through initiation, transformation, symbol, ritual, or unusual states.

M18 — Correspondence: different levels or domains are linked through correspondences, emanations, symbols, resonance, analogy, or participation.

M19 — Death / Persistence: consciousness or personhood is interpreted as persisting through death, transition, dissolution, reincarnation, ancestorhood, or transformation.

M20 — Cosmological Participation: human consciousness is part of a larger cosmological process rather than a purely local event.

M21 — Generativity / Manifestation: consciousness, mind, awareness, or an ultimate principle is described as generating, expressing, differentiating, or manifesting the world.

M22 — Self-Reference / Reflexivity: a process, consciousness, or ultimate principle folds back upon itself, recognizes itself, or contains recursive self-relation.

M23 — Transformation of State: consciousness can enter discrete or continuous states with qualitatively different organization.

M24 — Attention / Intention: direction of attention, will, intention, or focus changes the organization or experience of consciousness.

M25 — Practice / Discipline: consciousness can be investigated or transformed through repeatable disciplines.

## Evidence classes

P = primary textual claim
H = historical/anthropological claim
S = scholarly synthesis
E = empirical scientific observation
X = speculative / heterodox claim
O = project-original hypothesis

## Convergence scoring

No source gets a vote merely for being old or famous.

For each motif record independent source families, geographic diversity, temporal diversity, textual confidence, semantic confidence, and degree of interpretive compression.

The resulting score is a research prioritization score, not a truth probability.

## Required distinction

CONVERGENCE ≠ PROOF.

A motif recurring across many traditions is evidence that humans repeatedly articulated or experienced a similar structure. It is not by itself proof that the corresponding metaphysical proposition is objectively true.

The purpose is to discover the strongest shared model and make it experimentally vulnerable.
